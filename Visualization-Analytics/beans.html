BEANS (student proposal)

Assessment: ✅ Fit · Category: Visualization & Analytics · Assessed: 2026-09-21 · Status: awaiting instructor approval · Runner-up category: Education + Training

Proposal (as submitted): BEANS is a learning-oriented coffee databank web-app that educates newcomers on beans and preparation methods by preference. It cross-references brew details (bean type, grind size, method) to estimate the final taste; users log a brew + feedback, and the app suggests improvements/alternatives from the collective community databank. A relevancy search recommends coffees by preference + search history; there's a shop for beans/gear, monthly subscription tiers, and an inventory tracker (with nearby store/cafe suggestions). Named technical features: a Parametric Taste & Extraction Visualizer (brew variables → dynamic extraction-yield curves + a sensory taste-profile radar chart); a Brew Diagnostics & Dial-In Tool (qualitative feedback like sour/astringent/hollow → inverse parameter corrections); a Taste-Profile Catalog Search & Vector Ranking (multi-attribute search ranking beans by similarity to the user's taste + logs); and a high-throughput C++ vector search + neural re-ranking engine. Load-bearing C++: a compiled C++→WebAssembly module runs client-side computing particle-level solute diffusion / extraction dynamics in a fixed-timestep numerical loop, with slider inputs continuously re-injecting parameters to update the taste radar without frame drops or network hops; plus a dedicated C++ service for high-dimensional vector math (hardware SIMD distance across flavor profiles) and an embedded lightweight neural cross-encoder that re-ranks candidate beans before the API returns results. Tools: a Dial-In & Recipe Calibration Utility (author/test/tune brew parameters per bean; roasters author bean-specific templates with grind/temperature/ratio bounds + sensory target envelopes; a closed-loop diagnostic that ingests taste feedback, runs inverse heuristics, and previews the corrected radar before committing a versioned profile). Design features: estimate brew taste from inputs; a guide-me/recipe feature; a community to share experiments; and monetization (B2C bean/machine subscriptions; B2B roaster onboarding).

Note on category: filed under Visualization & Analytics — the load-bearing product is the interactive, data-moving visualization (extraction curves + taste radar recomputed live from slider inputs) plus vector-ranking analytics over a coffee databank; coffee brewing isn't claimed by another category, and the data moves (live recompute, ranking). Runner-up Education + Training (the "learning-oriented / guide-me" framing) — but there's no structured curriculum/competency, the mechanism is visualization + recommendation, so V&A is the better home. (Not CMS: roaster authoring is one tool, not the product; not Simulator: the extraction sim feeds the visualization, it isn't the whole product.)

Scope & risk notes (advisory, not blocking):

  1. Keep the load-bearing C++ on the team's side of the AI. The graded performant-C++ is the extraction WASM solver, the SIMD vector-search and the custom chart rendering — the "lightweight neural cross-encoder" should be a small, bounded component behind the C++ vector-search service (with the ranking working from the vector similarity even if the neural re-rank is swapped out), not the deliverable itself.
  2. Don't let the e-commerce become the product. The shop / subscription / B2B-onboarding / inventory features are legitimate but should stay light backend (Firebase + the databank) — the visualizer, the dial-in tool and the taste-vector ranking are the module deliverables; otherwise the project risks collapsing into CRUD + a store.

References:

  • Barista Hustle — "The Coffee Compass" (an interactive diagnostic that maps qualitative faults — sour, bitter, weak, strong — to concrete brew-parameter corrections: "extract more/less", "more/less coffee"; the direct precedent for the Dial-In & Diagnostics tool)
  • Coffee extraction — Wikipedia (the extraction-yield vs strength / TDS model and the brewing-control chart that relate grind/ratio/time/temperature to taste — the domain model the parametric visualizer estimates)
  • SCA — "How to Use the Coffee Taster's Flavor Wheel" (the standardized multi-attribute sensory lexicon/vocabulary behind the taste-profile radar + the vector matching)

How each requirement applies:

Platform fit: Main: Web browser on mobile — the stated architecture is a C++→WASM client (the extraction solver runs client-side in the browser), so the app is web-first and mobile-web friendly (device: Pixel 9a); the Native PC build is the development/tools vehicle (the dial-in calibration utility). Extended: Firebase (accounts, community databank, shop/subscription data) + a Linux server the team sets up by M2 (full use T2) hosting the dedicated C++ vector-search + neural re-ranking service.
Front end: Custom-rendered data views — the extraction-yield curves and the sensory **taste-profile radar**, recomputed live from slider inputs — plus the catalog/search results, the dial-in diagnostic view, community records and the shop UI. Languages: Web — HTML/JS/CSS + C++→WASM (Emscripten) for the solver + chart rendering (OpenGL ES / WebGL for the charts).
Back end: The client-side WASM extraction solver + the custom chart renderer, and a server-side **C++ vector-search service** (SIMD distance across flavor profiles + a bounded neural re-rank) over the coffee databank; plus Firebase for accounts, community logs and shop/subscription data. Languages: C++ (solver + vector service); Firebase-backed services + the Linux vector-service host on the Extended tier.
Backend scope: Server-indicated (T2) — the vector-search service + databank + shop seam set up & basic-tested by M2, full use in T2.
  • Loop and/or service: two load-bearing pieces — the client-side fixed-timestep WASM extraction solver (particle-level solute diffusion → dissolved solids / extraction yield → taste coordinates, recomputed live as sliders change) and the server-side C++ vector-search service (SIMD multi-attribute distance + a bounded neural re-rank) ranking beans by taste similarity.
  • Data-driven: beans, grind/temperature/ratio parameters, sensory attribute vocabularies, dial-in templates + sensory envelopes, and community logs are all data — a new bean or dial-in template is a data change (authored via the calibration tool).
  • 2D: the extraction-yield curves and the taste-profile radar are custom-rendered charts (no chart libraries) recomputed in real time — this is the 2D gate; plus the catalog/diagnostic/shop UI. 3D: not needed. Networking: the vector service + shop are async client↔︎server (Application Communication ✅), so a bespoke streaming Networking System is optional (🔵).
  • Custom tool: the Dial-In & Recipe Calibration Utility — roasters author bean templates (grind/temperature/ratio bounds + sensory envelopes) and the closed-loop diagnostic previews the corrected radar before committing a versioned profile.
  • External DB: the coffee databank, community logs, shop/subscription and inventory. Auth ⏭️: accounts (B2C users + B2B roasters). Analytics ⏭️: optional — the recommendation/search-history analytics (the team's own ranking is the deliverable via G10).

Track Goals & Team Composition

Recommended composition: 6 CS + 2 Design + 1 Art (9) — a data product lives on UX and information design: the explore/diagnose/recommend flows, the view/drill-down architecture, the metric/threshold (taste-model) design, the data storytelling and the shop/community UX are a large surface for two designers; the art surface is comparatively small (perceptual palette, chart styling, iconography, dense-view UI) and suits one artist (rules: min 1 Design mandated, max 9 total, D+A ≤ 3; see team-formation.pdf). If the databank/UX outgrows two designers, that is the place to add, not art. Scope note (advisory): a WASM extraction solver + a SIMD vector service + custom charts + a dial-in tool + a community databank + a shop is a large surface — the solver + the taste radar + the catalog vector-search + the dial-in tool is the M1/M2 floor; the neural re-rank, the full community and the shop/subscription/B2B are the stretch.
